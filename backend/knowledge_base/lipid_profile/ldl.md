## Overview

Low-density lipoprotein (LDL) is a type of lipoprotein that carries cholesterol through the bloodstream. LDL cholesterol is commonly referred to as "bad" cholesterol because higher levels of LDL cholesterol are associated with an increased risk of plaque buildup in the arteries.

## What the Test Is Used For

An LDL cholesterol measurement is part of a lipid profile or cholesterol test. It helps healthcare professionals evaluate cholesterol levels and assess cardiovascular risk together with other information.

A lipid profile may also include measurements such as total cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and triglycerides.

## Understanding the Result

The meaning of an LDL cholesterol result depends on the individual's overall health and cardiovascular risk factors.

The laboratory report may provide a reference range or interpretation for the measured value. Clinical interpretation may also consider factors such as age, medical history, existing cardiovascular disease, diabetes, blood pressure, smoking status, and other risk factors.

## High LDL Cholesterol

Higher LDL cholesterol levels can contribute to the buildup of cholesterol-containing plaque in arteries. Over time, this buildup may contribute to cardiovascular disease.

An elevated LDL result alone does not establish that a person has cardiovascular disease.

## Low LDL Cholesterol

Lower LDL cholesterol levels can occur in some people naturally or as a result of lifestyle factors, medical conditions, or cholesterol-lowering treatment.

The significance of a lower result depends on the individual's overall clinical context.

## Important Interpretation Note

LDL cholesterol should not be interpreted in isolation. A healthcare professional may consider the complete lipid profile, medical history, cardiovascular risk factors, and other relevant information.
