## Overview

The hemoglobin A1C test, also called HbA1c or A1C, is a blood test that provides information about a person's average blood glucose level over approximately the previous two to three months.

The test measures the percentage of hemoglobin that has glucose attached to it.

## What the Test Is Used For

The A1C test can be used to help diagnose type 2 diabetes and prediabetes and to monitor blood glucose control in people who have diabetes.

Healthcare professionals may use A1C results together with other information when evaluating a person's glucose status.

## Understanding the Result

An A1C result is reported as a percentage.

Higher A1C levels generally indicate higher average blood glucose levels over the preceding several months.

The interpretation of an A1C result depends on the clinical context and the testing method.

## Prediabetes and Diabetes

A1C results may fall into ranges associated with normal glucose levels, prediabetes, or diabetes.

However, a diagnosis should be made by a qualified healthcare professional using appropriate clinical information and testing procedures.

MedExplain should explain the reported result and its general meaning without independently diagnosing diabetes or prediabetes.

## Factors That Can Affect the Result

Certain medical conditions and circumstances can affect A1C measurements. Examples include some forms of anemia, conditions affecting red blood cells, kidney disease, liver disease, and some hemoglobin variants.

Therefore, an A1C result may need to be interpreted together with other laboratory information.

## Important Interpretation Note

An A1C result should not be interpreted in isolation. The patient's medical history, symptoms, other glucose measurements, laboratory information, and the reason for testing may all be relevant.
