## Overview

Hemoglobin is an iron-rich protein found in red blood cells. It carries oxygen from the lungs to the rest of the body. A hemoglobin test measures the amount of hemoglobin in a blood sample and is commonly included as part of a complete blood count (CBC).

## What the Test Is Used For

A hemoglobin test can help healthcare professionals evaluate the amount of hemoglobin in the blood and assess whether the result may be associated with conditions such as anemia or other blood-related conditions.

The test may also be used to monitor people who are being evaluated or treated for conditions that can affect hemoglobin levels.

## Understanding the Result

A laboratory report usually compares the measured hemoglobin value with a reference range.

Reference ranges can vary between laboratories and may depend on factors such as age and other characteristics. Therefore, the reference range printed on the patient's own laboratory report should be considered when interpreting the result.

An abnormal hemoglobin result does not by itself establish the cause of the abnormality.

## Low Hemoglobin

A lower-than-reference-range hemoglobin result can occur for several reasons. Examples include different types of anemia, iron deficiency, blood loss, certain nutritional deficiencies, and some other medical conditions.

A low result should therefore be considered together with other laboratory results, symptoms, medical history, and the laboratory's reference range.

## High Hemoglobin

A higher-than-reference-range hemoglobin result can also have several possible explanations. Examples include living at higher altitude, dehydration, smoking, and certain heart, lung, or blood-related conditions.

An elevated result does not by itself identify a specific disease.

## Important Interpretation Note

A hemoglobin result should not be interpreted in isolation. Healthcare professionals may consider the patient's symptoms, medical history, other blood-test results, and the reference range provided by the laboratory.
